# High-Yield Credit Analytics V2
## Literature Review — Theory and Empirical Foundations

### Purpose

This literature review evaluates the theoretical foundations relevant to
time-varying credit-market risk-bearing capacity and determines whether the
candidate construct is:

1. already established in the literature,
2. equivalent to an existing construct under different terminology,
3. a synthesis or extension of existing mechanisms, or
4. sufficiently distinct to support a new theoretical contribution.

The review will initially focus on the relationship among:

- intermediary constraints
- intermediary risk-bearing capacity
- aggregate investor risk-bearing capacity
- credit risk premia
- excess bond premia
- liquidity
- risk aversion
- financial conditions

---

## Research Question for the Literature Review

> How does existing asset-pricing and intermediary-finance theory explain
> time variation in the market's capacity to absorb risky corporate credit,
> and is aggregate credit-market risk-bearing capacity theoretically distinct
> from intermediary constraints, risk aversion, liquidity, and existing
> measures of credit risk premia?

## Candidate Theoretical Gap

Not yet established.

Do not claim a literature gap until the relevant literature has been
systematically reviewed.

Current candidate question:

> Existing intermediary-finance models may explain how particular financial
> constraints affect particular intermediaries. It remains to be determined
> whether an economically meaningful aggregate credit-market risk-bearing
> capacity can be defined across heterogeneous credit investors and whether
> such a state has distinct implications for corporate credit risk premia.

## He & Krishnamurthy (2012) — A Model of Capital and Crises

**Role in V2:** Foundational intermediary asset-pricing theory.

**Core mechanism:**  
Households rely on specialized financial intermediaries to obtain exposure to
risky assets. When intermediary capital becomes scarce, the sector's capacity
to bear risk falls and equilibrium risk premia can rise sharply.

**Relevant construct:**  
Risk-bearing capacity is linked to the capitalization/constraints of the
specialized intermediary sector rather than simply to household risk
preferences.

**Implication for V2:**  
The basic mechanism from intermediary capital scarcity to reduced
risk-bearing capacity and higher risk premia is established theory and cannot
constitute V2's theoretical contribution.

The model also clarifies an important modeling assumption: risky-asset
intermediation is concentrated in a specialized intermediary sector. V2 must
determine how later literature relaxes this structure for heterogeneous
institutional investors, particularly in corporate credit.

**Remaining question for V2:**  
How does risk-bearing capacity aggregate when multiple classes of credit
investors face different constraints and can imperfectly substitute for one
another?
