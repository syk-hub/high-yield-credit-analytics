# Kargar (2021) – Heterogeneous Intermediary Asset Pricing

## Role in V2

Extends representative intermediary asset pricing by allowing multiple
intermediary sectors with different risk-bearing capacities and financial
constraints. Demonstrates that the composition of intermediary capital affects
equilibrium risk premia.

---

## Core Mechanism

Traditional intermediary asset pricing assumes a representative intermediary.

Kargar relaxes this assumption by introducing heterogeneous intermediary
sectors with different capacities to absorb risk.

During normal periods, more aggressive intermediaries hold a larger share of
risky assets.

During financial stress, aggressive intermediaries become constrained and must
reduce risky positions. Less aggressive intermediaries become the marginal
buyers but require higher expected returns to absorb additional risk.

Therefore,

Changes in the composition of intermediary wealth—not merely aggregate
intermediary wealth—affect equilibrium risk premia.

---

## Relevant Construct

Two state variables summarize the intermediary sector.

**x**

Aggregate intermediary wealth.

Represents the overall financial health and aggregate risk-bearing capacity of
the intermediary sector.

**y**

Share of wealth held by aggressive intermediaries.

Represents the composition of intermediary capital and determines which
intermediary type becomes the marginal investor.

---

## Empirical Translation

Because the theoretical state variable cannot be observed directly, Kargar
constructs an empirical proxy.

He approximates the aggressive intermediary share using broker-dealer equity
relative to broker-dealer plus commercial-bank equity.

He finds that shocks to intermediary composition contain information beyond
aggregate intermediary capital.

---

## Main Contribution

Composition matters.

Two economies with identical aggregate intermediary capital can have different
risk premia if intermediary capital is distributed differently across
intermediary sectors.

---

## Implication for V2

Representative intermediary models are insufficient for understanding modern
credit markets.

V2 should recognize that different intermediary sectors possess different
risk-bearing capacities and constraints.

However, V2 does not directly observe intermediary balance sheets.

An open question is whether changing intermediary capacity can be inferred from
observable market variables.

---

## Remaining Question

Can market-based variables recover information about changing intermediary
risk-bearing capacity without directly observing intermediary balance sheets?
