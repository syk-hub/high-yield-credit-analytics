# Gilchrist & Zakrajšek (2012) – Credit Spreads and Business Cycle Fluctuations

## Role in V2

Foundational paper introducing the Excess Bond Premium (EBP), one of the core
variables used throughout the HY OAS V2 project.

---

## Core Mechanism

Observed corporate bond spreads combine multiple economic mechanisms.

Part of the spread compensates investors for expected default losses.

Another part reflects changing financial-market conditions and investors'
required compensation for bearing credit risk.

Using raw credit spreads therefore mixes expected default risk with changes in
financial-sector risk appetite.

---

## Why Ordinary Credit Spreads Are "Contaminated"

Observed credit spreads contain:

• expected default losses

plus

• additional compensation required by investors.

Because both components move over the business cycle, ordinary spreads cannot
cleanly identify financial-sector conditions.

---

## How Excess Bond Premium Is Constructed

Gilchrist & Zakrajšek first estimate the component of bond spreads explained by
issuer characteristics associated with expected default.

The remaining unexplained portion is defined as the Excess Bond Premium.

Conceptually,

Observed Spread

=

Expected Default Component

+

Excess Bond Premium

---

## Economic Interpretation

Expected default reflects the borrower's financial condition.

The Excess Bond Premium reflects the willingness and ability of financial
intermediaries and investors to bear credit risk.

During recessions,

• firms become riskier,

and

• investors require additional compensation beyond expected default losses.

The EBP captures this second mechanism.

---

## Main Contribution

Separates credit spreads into

1. borrower default risk

and

2. financial-sector pricing of risk.

This decomposition provides a cleaner measure of credit-market conditions than
raw credit spreads alone.

---

## Implication for V2

EBP should not be interpreted as another credit spread.

Instead, it represents a theoretically motivated decomposition of credit
pricing that isolates financial-sector risk compensation.

V2 should therefore treat HY OAS and EBP as complementary rather than
redundant variables.

---

## Remaining Question

Can observable market variables explain changes in the Excess Bond Premium, and
to what extent does EBP proxy for changing intermediary risk-bearing capacity?
